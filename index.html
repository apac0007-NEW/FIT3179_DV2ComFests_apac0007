FIT3179 Data Visualisation 2 · Community festivals in Australia

Who makes the party happen?

Street parties, markets and neighbourhood festivals only happen because someone gives their time. This page follows that thread in six short chapters: first across Australia, then through one council’s events calendar in Brisbane.

  • 22.6%of Australians aged 15 and over volunteered through an organisation in 2025. In 2010 it was 36.2%.
  • 30 of 1,998events on Brisbane’s council calendar are festivals or markets (1.5%).
  • 87%of those festival and market listings are free to attend.
01

Australia · volunteering

Fewer people are giving their time

Festivals, markets and street parties run on volunteers. The Australian Bureau of Statistics asks about it in its General Social Survey. In 2025 about 22.6% of people aged 15 and over, more than 5 million Australians, volunteered through an organisation. Five years earlier it was 25.0%.

Chapter 1 · where

Every state and territory has a smaller share volunteering than in 2020

Tasmania is highest at 27.5% and Victoria lowest at 21.8%. The ACT fell furthest, by 8.9 percentage points (a percentage point is one step on the percentage scale). The diamond marks Brisbane, the city we visit in chapters 4 to 6.

Chapter 1 · ranking

The ACT went from first to joint sixth

Because the ACT fell so far, the league table reshuffled. Tasmania moved up to 1st. New South Wales fell the least (0.8 points), so it climbed from last to 5th.

Hover over a dot to follow one state across the five years.

Chapter 1 · over time

Down by more than a third in 15 years

Formal volunteering has fallen in every survey since 2010, from 36.2% to 22.6%. Informal help for neighbours and friends has slipped more slowly: 33.4% in 2019 and 29.5% in 2025.

Formal means unpaid work through an organisation. Informal means helping neighbours and friends.

−13.6percentage points in formal volunteering since 2010

In short

Volunteering is falling everywhere, and fastest where it used to be strongest. Fewer volunteers means fewer hands for festivals.

02

Australia · who volunteers

Small communities and middle age carry the load

The same survey shows who steps forward. Local celebrations often rely on exactly these groups.

Chapter 2 · place

The further from a big city, the more people volunteer

In remote areas 34.0% volunteer through an organisation, compared with 20.9% in major cities. Where communities are small, a bigger share of people pitch in.

34.0%volunteer formally in remote areas, against 20.9% in major cities

Chapter 2 · age and gender

Middle age carries the load

People aged 35 to 54 volunteer most. Women lead at 35 to 44 (30.5% against 24.9% for men) and men lead at 45 to 54 (30.6% against 26.8%).

The late twenties are the quietest years, at about 13% for both men and women.

In short

Festival organisers lean on people in their thirties to fifties and on small communities. These are the same groups who say they are short of time (chapter 3).

03

Australia · connection

People join for people, and many wait to be asked

The survey does not ask about festivals directly. It does show what draws people into community groups, and what keeps them out. This is where connection shows up.

Chapter 3 · what for

Sport comes first, and women lead in community and cultural groups

Community or cultural groups, the type closest to festival organisers, are third for both men and women. Women are more likely to help them (20.1% against 15.2%).

Chapter 3 · why

Three of the top six reasons are about people

Helping the community is the top reason (65.2%). Personal or family involvement (31.6%) and social contact (29.1%) also rank in the top six. Teal bars are reasons about connecting with people.

Half of volunteers started because someone they knew was involved or asked them (50.3%).

50.3%of volunteers began because someone they knew was involved or asked them

Chapter 3 · what stops people

“No time” is the barrier for 25 to 54-year-olds. Young people say nobody asked

More than 56% of non-volunteers aged 25 to 54 say they have no time. Among 15 to 24-year-olds, 22.1% say they were not sure how, or never asked, the highest share of any age group.

Magenta outlines mark these two patterns.

In short

Connection is the pull: people give time for the community and for each other, and an invitation is often what starts it. Young people are the group least likely to have been asked.

Part 2

From the national picture to one council’s events calendar

04

Brisbane · where

Festivals are a small slice of a very busy calendar

Now we zoom in on one council. Brisbane City Council lists 1,998 events from 29 October to 19 November 2026. Each listing is one session, and many repeat: there are only 426 different titles. We count festivals, markets, food and Christmas events as celebrations. That is 30 listings, or 1.5%.

Chapter 4 · where

Celebrations cluster in the city centre and in parks

28 of the 30 mapped festival and market events are within 10 km of the city centre. The busiest festival venue is Queen Street Mall (5 events). 53% of festival and market events are in parks.

In short

The city centre dominates: one hexagon holds 15% of all mapped events. Festivals and markets spread a little further, into parks such as Roma Street Parkland and the City Botanic Gardens.

05

Brisbane · diversity

Is there something for everyone?

Diversity shows whether different people can find something to do in the same place. We count how many of the eight kinds of event each area hosts.

Chapter 5 · where

Only 4 of 58 busy areas host five or more kinds of event

We keep hexagons with at least 10 events. 41 of the 58 host just one or two kinds. The few that host five or more are all within 4 km of the city centre.

Hover over a hexagon to see its count.

Chapter 5 · what

Fitness and library sessions are 80% of the calendar

Fitness and wellbeing (49%) and library events (31%) make up about four in five listings. Festivals and markets are 1.5%.

Chapter 5 · which spaces

Libraries and parks do different jobs

619 of the 619 events at library venues are library events. Parks mostly host fitness & wellbeing sessions (518 of 656).

Council venues host 171 of the 216 arts and culture events.

Chapter 5 · who for

Seniors are named in more events than any other group

Seniors are named in 813 events. Children and teenagers are named in 181 to 272 events each. An event can name more than one group, so bars overlap.

In short

There is something for most people, but the mix leans towards older adults and weekday sessions. Festivals and markets are the exception.

06

Brisbane · time and cost

When events happen, and what it takes to join

Connection needs events people can actually get to. Timing, cost and booking all decide who turns up.

Chapter 6 · when

Festivals are the weekend exception: 57% start on a weekend

Only 23% of all events start on a weekend. For festivals and markets it is 57%. The busiest day of the whole calendar is Thursday 12 November, with 121 events.

57%of festival and market events start on a weekend, against 23% of all events

Chapter 6 · time of day

About 45% of events start at 9 or 10 in the morning

The busiest single slot is Thu at 9:00. A weekday-morning calendar suits people who are free in the daytime. It is harder for the 25 to 54-year-olds who told the survey they have no time.

Chapter 6 · the mix

Fitness and library sessions are the base of every day

Fitness and library sessions make up most of the calendar on weekdays and weekends. Festivals and markets are a thin band, but a thicker one at weekends: 4% of weekend events, against 1% of weekday events.

Click a kind in the legend to follow it.

Chapter 6 · cost

Most events are free: 74% overall, 87% for festivals and markets

Each square is 1 in every 100 events. Free events remove one barrier to joining in.

Chapter 6 · booking

Booking is the bigger hurdle: 17% of events ask for it

Workshops and tours ask for booking most often (56%), then arts and culture events (43%). Only 13% of festival and market listings ask people to book, so they stay easy to drop in on.

In short

Festivals and markets are free, mostly at weekends and in open spaces, which is when and where most people with busy weekdays can join in.

What it adds up to

Festivals run on people, and people join when they are asked

  1. 1

    Fewer volunteers, everywhere

    Formal volunteering fell from 36.2% to 22.6% in 15 years, and every state and territory fell between 2020 and 2025.

  2. 2

    Connection is the pull

    Social contact and personal ties rank among the top reasons to volunteer, and half of volunteers began because someone they knew was involved or asked them.

  3. 3

    Festivals are rare, but open

    In Brisbane only 30 of 1,998 listings are festivals or markets. They are 87% free, 57% on weekends and 53% in parks: the easiest events to drop in on.

Next time you enjoy a local festival, ask who is behind it, and whether they need a hand.

Sources, method and author

Data source 1: volunteering. Australian Bureau of Statistics (ABS), General Social Survey 2025, released 6 May 2026, as reported in Volunteering Australia, Volunteering Statistics: General Social Survey 2025 (May 2026), which reproduces ABS Tables 6.3, 7.3, 10.3, 11.3 and 13.3. People aged 15 and over. Formal volunteering means unpaid work through an organisation. The author typed the tables into JSON files. Results are survey estimates, and small differences may not be meaningful.

Data source 2: events. Brisbane City Council, Events dataset (brisbane-city-council-events), data.brisbane.qld.gov.au, downloaded 29 October 2026. The export holds the next 2,000 listed events; two long-running exhibitions were removed, leaving 1,998. Events without coordinates are left out of the maps. Kinds of event are grouped by the author from the council’s own tags: “festivals & markets” covers festivals, markets, food and Christmas events. The data shows what is listed, not how many people attend.

Boundaries. State and territory outlines come from a simplified GeoJSON copy of the ABS Australian Statistical Geography Standard state and territory boundaries (ABS data, licensed CC BY 4.0). The Brisbane maps use the same outlines as a coastline guide, so rivers and small islands are not shown.

Tools and AI use. Charts are drawn with Vega-Lite. Hexagon bins were computed in Python. AI tools were used to help write code and text and to check the design against the assignment brief.

Author: Aditya Pachegaonkar, FIT3179 Data Visualisation 2, Monash University, 29 October 2026. The Vega-Lite specification for every chart is a readable .json file in this repository, next to the data files it loads.
